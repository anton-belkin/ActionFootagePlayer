For divers and snorkelers

Steady under water. On everyone's phone tonight.

Your camera's stabilization is tuned for land. Under water, turn it off, and Action Footage Player steadies the dive while it plays, from the camera's own motion data, in the light you saw, with your depth on screen. Mark the turtle, press one key, and it's ready to AirDropsend to everyone's phone before dinner.

Why dive footage disappoints

The camera got the shot. Getting it back is the hard part.

It wobbles, even with stabilization on

In-camera stabilization is tuned for land. Under water the clip still wobbles, likely because water bends light and the lens then acts like a longer one. Every diver has seen it.

Grey, or flattened

Shoot in log and every clip comes out grey. Shoot in the normal mode and the bright surface and the dark reef get squeezed into one flat picture.

Fixing it takes an editor and an evening

Color and stabilization usually mean editing software, a project per clip and long renders. Most dives never get them.

The turtle is somewhere in an hour of blue

By the time the clips are copied and you've found the right minute, the group has moved on.

The evening after the dive

Double-click the clip. It plays the way the dive looked.

No import, no grading, nothing to render first. It all happens while the clip plays, and nothing changes your originals unless you ask.

  1. Steady, under water too

    With the camera's stabilization off, the player steadies the clip from the motion the camera recorded, corrected for how water bends light. The color fringes water adds at the edges of the frame go too.

  2. The light you saw, no grading

    Log footage plays in HDR straight away: the bright surface and the shadows on the reef both keep their detail. No look is added; the exposure stays as you shot it.

  3. Your depth on screen

    On the Osmo Action 6, the depth and whether the camera is in the water show over the video. In a housing the camera can't measure depth, but the in-water status still shows.

  4. Jump straight to the turtle

    Press the camera's highlight button during the dive, and the marks show on the seek bar. Ctrl+] jumps to the next one.

  5. A clean still of the moment

    Pause: a moment later the frame is rebuilt from the frames around it, so the sensor's noise from the dim light under water mostly goes. Ctrl+p turns it off, and on again.

  6. Keep the moments that matter

    Press l at the start, l at the end, then k, and give it a name. It's kept as a small file in the trip's _Moments folder, right there in FinderFile Explorer, in color. The first time, you pick the trip's folder in the side panel's Project tab.

  7. Share it the same evening

    Shift+E turns the part into one file, steady and in color, that iPhones and Macs show right. Exported on a Mac in the HLG+DV look, the one clips start in on a Mac screen without HDR, it also carries Dolby Vision information, which iPhones, iPads and Macs use to show it well on screens without HDR too.

Measured, not promised

Steady, and measured.

The loop is the proof: the same dive as the camera recorded it and as it plays here. The default settings are made for water, so there's nothing to set up.

Before your next dive

Two settings on the camera. One double-click after.

Set them once, and every dive after plays steady and in color.

  1. Set the color profile to log

    D-Log M on DJI. GP-Log on GoPro HERO12 Black (firmware 1.20 or later) and HERO13 Black. 10-bit I-Log on Insta360, which you then pick in the player with Ctrl+l: 8-bit I-Log isn't supported. GoPro's Flat isn't log, and HERO8 to HERO11 have no log. No log on your camera? Skip this step: the stabilization still works.

  2. Turn the camera's stabilization off

    EIS on DJI, HyperSmooth on GoPro, FlowState on Insta360. On DJI, keep the Wide field of view too: only then does the camera record its motion. A clip the camera has already steadied plays as it is.

  3. Copy the clips straight from the card

    An app that trims or exports clips drops the motion data the player steadies them with.

  4. Double-click a clip

    That's it.

White balance: set it as you always do. The player shows what was recorded.

Questions divers ask

Before you download.

I only snorkel. Is it for me?

Yes, if anything more. Near the surface the contrast between sunlight and shadow is at its strongest, and the camera moves with every wave. Both are what Action Footage Player is built for.

I shoot Flat. Is that log?

No. GoPro's Flat is a flatter picture, not a log recording, so it plays as recorded, without the HDR conversion. GoPro's log is GP-Log, on the HERO12 Black (firmware 1.20 or later) and HERO13 Black; the HERO8 to HERO11 have none.

Flat clips shot with HyperSmooth off still play steady.

Does it work in a dive housing?

Yes. The stabilization and the color work the same. In a housing the camera can't measure depth, as its pressure sensor feels only the air inside, so no depth shows. On the Osmo Action 6, whether the camera is in the water still shows.

What about the shots above water?

On the Osmo Action 6, the correction for water follows the camera's own in-water status, so a clip that goes in and out of the water is corrected only where it's in.

Above water, the camera's own stabilization works well, so you can leave it on there. For a clip shot in air with it off, Ctrl+u switches the correction for water off.

Do I need an HDR screen?

No, though it helps. Recent iPhones, MacBook Pros, HDR TVs and monitors show everything log recorded. On Apple devices a dive also looks good on an ordinary screen, in the player and in the clips you export. On some other phones, computers and TVs, an exported clip can look a little flatter.

The stabilization and the depth readout help on any screen.

Does it bring back the reds lost at depth?

No. Water absorbs red light before it reaches the camera, and Action Footage Player shows what was recorded, with its full range. Getting reds back takes a red filter, a dive light or white balance on the camera, or color correction afterwards.

How does a dive look in HDR here?

As the camera recorded it: the exposure stays as you shot it, so the brightness doesn't shift as you swim, and the shadows on the reef keep their detail.

Its exports have no look added either, so you can still adjust them to your taste. A look baked into a file can't be taken out.

Will my old dive videos look better?

Clips shot with EIS off and the Wide field of view play steady straight from the folder: nothing is rendered or copied. Clips shot in log play in HDR. Clips shot in the normal mode with stabilization on look as they always did: Action Footage Player can't add what wasn't recorded.

Does it change my original files?

Only if you ask. To cut a spoiled part out of a clip (Ctrl+x), the player rewrites the clip without it, with nothing re-encoded, and the original goes to the Trash. Otherwise your originals stay as they are.

Exports go to the trip's _Exports folder, the moments you keep to _Moments; your marks are a small file beside the clip.

Is there a Windows version?

Yes, for Windows 10 and 11. The Mac app is the one in daily use; the Windows app works too, but it's an alpha with little testing so far.

Is it free?

Yes, open source under GPL-3.0. Nothing is locked, and there is no account to make.
