Stabilization

Shaky clips play steady. Nothing to render first.

Shot with the camera's own stabilization off? Double-click the clip, and it plays steady from the motion the camera recorded, while you watch. Stabilizing never changes the original file.

How it works

What it takes to steady a clip.

A shaky clip is the camera turning a little, many times a second. To steady it, each frame is turned back by as much as the camera turned. That takes three things: how the camera moved, how its lens draws the picture, and a program that puts the two together.

How the camera moved

Most action cameras have a gyroscope: a sensor that records, hundreds of times a second, how the camera turned. That's the clip's gyro data, or motion data, written into the video file. It's exact, and reading it takes no time. You get it when the camera's own stabilization is off: what to set is just below.

No gyro data? From the picture

A clip without gyro data can still be steadied: the motion is then found in the picture itself, by following details from frame to frame (optical flow). It's slower, so it's done once, ahead of time, and saved.

How the lens draws the picture

An action camera's wide lens bends the picture, more toward the edges. To turn a frame back the right way, the program needs that lens's shape: its profile. Then straight lines stay straight instead of wobbling at the edges.

The program: Gyroflow

The program that does it here is Gyroflow, free and open source. Its engine runs inside the player while the clip plays, so there's nothing to render first. It knows the lenses of most action cameras. Gyroflow is also an app of its own, for the clips that need more: the player opens a clip in it with Ctrl+e, and plays what you save there.

Before you shoot

What to set on the camera.

The camera records gyro data only in some modes, and the player uses it only when the camera says its own stabilization was off.

  1. Turn the camera's own stabilization off

    EIS (electronic image stabilization) on DJI, HyperSmooth on GoPro, FlowState in the camera on Insta360. On DJI, it has to be EIS off itself: a horizon mode, or EIS off with a crop, counts as on.

  2. On DJI, shoot in the Wide FOV

    DJI cameras record the gyro data with EIS off and the Wide field of view. In other FOVs they may record none, and those clips need Gyroflow's optical flow.

  3. Under water, keep it off. Above water, your choice

    In air, DJI's EIS works well, so there's little reason to turn it off there. Under water it doesn't, as of today's firmware, likely because water bends light and the lens then behaves like a longer one than EIS is tuned for. The preset that comes with the player is made for water. For more on dives, see Under water.

  4. Then double-click a clip

    Nothing else to set. Log or not doesn't matter here: color is a separate step, explained in Log footage.

Checked on real clips: DJI Osmo Action 6 with EIS off and GoPro HERO12 Black with HyperSmooth off, on a Mac with Apple Silicon. Other DJI cameras, other GoPros and single-lens Insta360 cameras are built for and should work. 360 clips: 360 video.

Gyroflow projects

Open it in Gyroflow. Save. It's back, stabilized.

Ctrl+e pauses the clip and opens it in Gyroflow, the app, for anything the player's settings don't do. Gyroflow has to be installed.

A clip steadied from its gyro data

The player first writes a project next to the clip, as it plays: the same preset, stabilization, level, horizon lock, lens and frame, and the motion data, so Gyroflow doesn't read it again. A .gyroflow that's already there is opened as it is, never overwritten.

A clip with a project

Its project opens in Gyroflow as it is. From then on the project sets the smoothing, the horizon, the frame, the projection, the turn and the lens under water: the panel shows its values, and you change them in Gyroflow.

No gyro data? No problem

The video itself opens in Gyroflow, which stabilizes it optically: it finds the motion in the picture itself (optical flow). The panel shows “Waiting for the project from Gyroflow…”. Save the project next to the clip, and the clip loads again where it was, stabilized.

Many clips at once: Stabilize optically in Action Footage Library does them one after another in the background.

On Windows, opening a video that has no project needs Gyroflow's path in gyroflow_app= in gyroflow.conf.

Each save comes back

Each time you save in Gyroflow (Cmd+SCtrl+S), the player reloads the stabilization at the same spot in the clip. From then on the clip plays with its project.

The video file itself is never written to: the project is always the .gyroflow file beside it.

The Transform tab

The settings, in the panel beside the video.

The side panel opens on its Project tab. How a clip is shown, steadied and framed is on its Transform tab; how to read the file, such as the turn it was recorded with and the lens under water, is on its Source tab, below. Tab hides the panel and shows it again. The Transform tab, from top to bottom:

Transform defined in
A card at the top says where what plays comes from: this session (nothing saved for it), the source, the moment or the clip preview. Under it: Save (it says Save to the source, Save to the moment or Save to the clip preview), Revert to saved, Use as default and the Auto save switch (default off). While a moment or clip preview plays, the switch Settings of its own is there too. Each setting below shows your choice filled and the saved one with a dot in its corner. What these do: How long a setting lasts.
Stabilization default On Ctrl+g
On: zoomed in so no edge shows. Wider: no zoom, so you see more of the picture and the edges show at times, filled as Edges says. Off: not stabilized; the lens below still applies. No motion data in the clip? It's greyed at Off, with Stabilize in Gyroflow… (Ctrl+e, above), and the rest of the tab stays. With a Gyroflow project, Wider and Off are for viewing only, never saved: Wider shows how much the project's crop cuts off, Off the motion as recorded.
Level default Medium Ctrl+k
How strongly. Weak: 10 % cropped, smoothness 50 %. Medium: 20 % cropped, smoothness 75 %. Strong: 30 % cropped, smoothness 100 %. When the camera turns further than the crop has room for, the picture follows it sooner, so edges show only then. Switching level takes effect at once, while the clip plays. Greyed when Stabilization is Off. Not shown for a clip with a project: it has the project's smoothing.
Horizon default Free
Level: the horizon kept level. It needs more zoom, so edges show more. Free: not kept level. The default is the preset's, and the one that comes with the player leaves the horizon free. Not shown for a clip with a project.
Lens default Corrected, Linear
Corrected: the lens's bend taken out, as its profile says, then drawn as you pick: Linear (straight lines), Fisheye 1 (stereographic), Fisheye 2 (equidistant), Fisheye 3 (equisolid) or Fisheye 4 (orthographic). As recorded: the lens as the camera recorded it. The lens is its own setting: with Stabilization Off and the lens Corrected, only the lens is corrected and nothing is smoothed. It needs a lens profile: an unknown lens stays at As recorded (Source › Lens says why). With a project it's As in the project, and As recorded is for viewing only.
Frame default Own, As is
The shape of the whole clip: Own (the video's), 16:9, 4:3, 5:4 or 1:1, then As is, Landscape or Portrait; in Portrait the ratios read 9:16, 3:4 and 4:5. It plays in it, and exports, moments, clip previews and photos are made in it. Zoom stays Gyroflow's. On a clip that isn't stabilized, mpv's own crop does it. With motion data, Make a Gyroflow project… (Ctrl+e) takes the frame into a new project. With a project, the card In the Gyroflow project shows its smoothing, horizon, frame and projection instead, with Edit in Gyroflow….
Water: Reduce color aberration default on Ctrl+a
Under water, the flat glass in front of the lens splits colors apart towards the frame's edges. This puts them back, wherever the lens is under water. On Osmo Action 6 dive clips, red's fringe at the edge goes from 3-7 px to about 1 px. It's part of the transform, saved with the rest. Playback only: exports keep the fringes. Greyed when Source › Under water, flat port is No.
Temporal denoise: Clean the noise default on Ctrl+p
The noise of dim light taken out while the clip plays, and paused: each frame is averaged with the frames on each side of it, each moved onto it by the motion between them, so the noise goes and the detail stays. The picture shows a few frames later, and the sound waits for it. Stronger, for a very noisy clip (off): more frames taken in, no grain kept, dark specks out and flat areas smoothed: open water clean, fine texture softer. Part of the transform, so it's kept per clip. Nothing is written into the files: exports play as recorded. How much of it this computer does is in Performance & Quality.
Sideways jerks Ctrl+j
Up close, a hand's small sideways jitter shakes the picture however well the camera's turns are taken out. Measure for this clip measures it once, from the camera's accelerometer and the picture, and keeps it; from then on the picture is moved against it as it plays. On an Osmo Action 6 clip, measuring takes about half the clip's length. For a clip that's stabilized.

The Source tab: how to read the clip

What the file is, set once for each source. The Input card at its top says “Changed for viewing. The dot marks what's saved”, “Saved in the source, for all its moments and clips” or “Nothing saved: as the file says”. Save to the source keeps what you changed, for the clip and all its moments and clip previews; Revert to saved goes back. It works from a moment or a clip preview too.

Recorded turned default As is
As is, 90° ↻, 180° or 90° ↺: for a clip shot with the camera turned, such as an Osmo Action 6 held upright. It plays, stabilizes and exports upright. When the file says it's turned, it's shown upright already, and these turn it more. The turn comes before the stabilization.
Lens
Camera, Profile (from the file, from the project, or unknown) and Motion data: what it's stabilized with, “Gyroflow project”, “Gyro, in-camera stabilization off”, “none: the camera stabilized it (EIS)” or “none”. Without a lens profile the lens can't be corrected; Gyroflow can set one (Ctrl+e), and picking one here comes later.
Under water, flat port default Auto Ctrl+u
The correction for how water bends light. Auto: as the camera's telemetry says, switching over one frame when a clip goes in or out. Only the Osmo Action 6 records that; for other cameras Auto takes the preset's (the shipped preset: under water). Yes: under water behind a flat port, the camera's own glass or a flat housing. No: above water, or behind a dome port. Greyed with a project, and for an unknown lens.

With a Gyroflow project, the turn and the lens under water are the project's: the tab says “Turn and lens as defined in the Gyroflow project”. Edit in Gyroflow… (Ctrl+e) is right under them. The log curve is set here too: Log footage.

How long a setting lasts

The Source tab's input is the clip's own: a change is for viewing until Save to the source, and it isn't carried to the next clip. The Transform tab's settings are kept together, as a whole, never one alone. What plays is one of these, the first that exists, never a mix:

  1. A moment's or a clip preview's own, while Settings of its own is on.
  2. The source's, saved in <clip>.afp.json beside the clip, with its marks.
  3. The session's: it starts as the defaults and follows what you change on clips with nothing saved.
  4. The defaults, as Use as default last wrote them.

On a clip with nothing saved, a change goes into the session's, so it sticks for the next clips too. On a clip with a saved transform, a change is for viewing: the dot shows what's saved, and Revert to saved goes back. Save writes what plays, whole, where the card says: the moment or clip preview when Settings of its own is on, else the source. Auto save, for this session, saves a clip or moment that has nothing saved as it opens, then each change. Use as default keeps what plays for the next start, and for the next clips now.

A Gyroflow project owns its settings: the smoothing, the horizon, the frame, the projection, the turn and the lens under water. The player shows them and never writes them. A key for one of them says it's “as defined in the Gyroflow project” and that Ctrl+e edits it in Gyroflow. What was saved for the clip before stays in its file and plays again if the project goes.

The preset, and yours

A clip without a project starts from Gyroflow's default preset with the player's own on top. That one is made for under water: the lens corrected for water (refraction 1.33) and the horizon left free. The Level replaces its smoothing and zoom. On a test clip, the correction for water took the leftover jitter between frames from 0.16 px to 0.02 px.

To start from your own, save a preset from Gyroflow (Export → Create settings preset) as default.gyroflow in ~/Library/Application Support/ActionFootagePlayer/gyroflow/%LOCALAPPDATA%\ActionFootagePlayer\gyroflow\. The panel's settings still go on top of it.

What goes into an export

Shift+E exports the clip as it plays: the same project or preset, the source's input (log curve, recorded turn, lens under water) and the transform: Stabilization, Level, Horizon, the lens corrected or as recorded and its projection, the frame's ratio and orientation, and the edge fill. Color-aberration removal is playback only. Exporting, and where the file goes, is on Log footage.

Edges

When the steadied frame doesn't cover the window.

In the Wider view, or when the camera turns further than the crop allows, parts of the window are empty. The player fills them, blurred and blended into the picture.

Trail by direction default
What was last seen in that direction, known from the gyro data, taken from the whole frame the camera recorded, including what the zoom crops away. It stays put as the camera pans. Where nothing was seen yet, the colors around spread in, a little darker and greyer.
Frame's edge spread out
Only the current frame's own colors, spread out from its edge. Nothing from earlier frames, so nothing that can be off, and the lightest for the computer.
Background color
The project's background color, as Gyroflow renders it.

Ctrl+b switches between them while the clip plays. They're in the panel's Performance & Quality tab, below. The Wider view picks a field of view where the steadied picture covers about as much area as the frame, so it shows more of the scene, with edges throughout.

The trail knows only how the camera turned, not how it moved. Near things the camera passed, and things that moved by themselves, can be off in it. Where nothing was seen, the fill is made-up color, not picture.

When it can't keep up, it asks

If more than a tenth of the frames are dropped for over 2 seconds, playback pauses and asks: “Stabilization can't keep up, frames are dropped. Play lighter:”. It offers what helps on that clip: 30 or 15 fps, a lighter edge fill, no edge fill, rendering at 1080p, skipping the color aberration correction on this screen, or playing on as it is, and “Don't ask again”. What you pick is kept.

On an M1 Pro MacBook Pro, the stabilization keeps up with 4K at 60 fps with room to spare. On a thin 15 W Windows laptop, 4K at 60 fps with the trail kept up at 15 fps, not at 30.

Moments

A moment keeps the transform it was saved with.

A moment is a part of a clip you keep (k). It takes what plays, whole: its stabilization, level, horizon, color aberration, lens, projection and frame. If the clip had a Gyroflow project, the moment also gets its own trimmed copy, _Moments/<name>.gyroflow, which then decides its horizon, crop and smoothing. A moment never changes by itself: a later change to the clip or to its project doesn't reach it. How to read the file, the Source tab's input, stays the source's, for all its moments.

While a moment plays, the Transform tab's card says the moment, and Settings of its own is on. What you change is for viewing until Save to the moment (or Auto save) writes it. Switch Settings of its own off, and the moment plays as its source; its own settings go, and its own project copy goes to the TrashRecycle Bin. The Project tab's switch Stabilized on its own smooths it from the moment's own motion only, or, off, with the clip's motion around it. A clip preview has the same Settings of its own switch, off at first.

Moments, in Organizing

How it decides

Three questions, in this order, each time a clip opens.

The player never guesses. It stabilizes a clip only when it knows how, and otherwise doesn't smooth it.

  1. A Gyroflow project next to the clip wins

    A Gyroflow project is a small .gyroflow file with everything Gyroflow needs to steady one clip. The player looks for one beside the video with the same name: DJI_0007_D.gyroflow or DJI_0007_D.MP4.gyroflow. If it finds one, the clip plays as Gyroflow renders it: the project's smoothing, zoom, lens, projection, output size, turn, horizon lock and correction for water. The player's Level doesn't apply.

  2. Else, the camera's gyro data, if the camera's stabilization was off

    Before the clip plays, the player reads what the camera wrote into the file. It steadies the clip only when the file has gyro data and the camera recorded that its own stabilization was off: EIS off on DJI (and the Wide FOV, the field of view), HyperSmooth off on GoPro, FlowState off on Insta360. GoPro and Insta360 clips also need their lens in Gyroflow's lens database. The clip then plays with the preset that comes with the player and the transform: the one saved for it, else yours on the Transform tab.

  3. Else, no gyro data? No problem: Ctrl+e opens it in Gyroflow

    Until then nothing is smoothed. A clip whose lens profile is known gets its lens corrected; a DJI clip shot with EIS on records none and plays as it was recorded. Press Ctrl+e, and the video opens in Gyroflow, which stabilizes it optically, from the motion in the picture itself (optical flow). Save the project there, next to the clip, and the clip loads again where it was, stabilized: it's now a clip with a project, case 1.

Why so strict: footage the camera has already steadied gets worse when it's stabilized a second time. So a clip whose camera says its stabilization was on, or doesn't say, isn't smoothed again. A wrong guess about a camera means its clips play as they are, not worse.

Limits

What it doesn't do.

  • No gyro data? Gyroflow stabilizes it optically. A DJI clip shot with EIS on, or in another FOV than Wide, may have none. Ctrl+e opens it in Gyroflow; save the project there, and it plays steady here. For many clips, Stabilize optically in Action Footage Library.
  • Camera stabilization on means as recorded, GoPro HyperSmooth and Insta360 FlowState included, even though those cameras record gyro data anyway.
  • The Transform tab says “No motion data in this clip” for any clip it doesn't stabilize, also a GoPro clip with HyperSmooth on that has gyro data. Gyroflow may then use that gyro data rather than optical flow.
  • Checked on two cameras: the Osmo Action 6 and the GoPro HERO12 Black. GoPro and Insta360 clips need their lens in Gyroflow's lens database.
  • A project made with Ctrl+e smooths a little differently in Gyroflow than the Level does in the player.
  • After a jump past what's been read, a clip without a project plays uncorrected for a moment (about 2 s from a network drive), then the correction fades in.
  • Color-aberration removal is playback only, not in exports.
  • No lens profile? Gyroflow sets one. Without one the lens can't be corrected; picking a profile in the player comes later.
  • While a clip plays, an export nearly stops. Pause to let it run.
  • Only files on a drive are stabilized, not streams from a web address.
  • The Windows app is an alpha, with little testing so far.

Questions

About stabilization.

Why does my clip still play shaky?

Most often the camera's own stabilization was on, or, on DJI, the clip wasn't shot in the Wide FOV, so there's no gyro data to use. The Transform tab says “No motion data in this clip”, and Source › Lens › Motion data says why. Press Ctrl+e to stabilize it in Gyroflow; once the project is saved next to the clip, it plays steady here.

Should I turn the camera's stabilization off above water too?

Not necessarily. In air, DJI's EIS works well. Under water it doesn't, and that's where turning it off pays. For a clip shot in air with EIS off on a camera other than the Osmo Action 6, set Source › Under water, flat port to No (Ctrl+u) and press Save to the source: the shipped preset assumes water.

Do I need Gyroflow?

Not for clips with gyro data and the camera's stabilization off: the player steadies them on its own. You need the Gyroflow app to make or change a project, and for clips without gyro data.

Does it change my video files?

No. The player only reads them. A project is a separate .gyroflow file next to the clip, and the player never overwrites one that's there.
