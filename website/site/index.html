Your action-camera clips, in color and steady. Just double-click.

How it works

One camera setting, one double-click, one key.

By default, your camera squeezes bright skies and deep shadows so a clip looks fine on any screen. Its log setting keeps all of it, but the clips come out grey, and making them look right used to take editing software. Action Footage Player does it as you press play.

  1. Set the camera once

    Choose log as the color setting: D-Log M on DJI, GP-Log on GoPro, 10-bit I-Log on Insta360 (in the player, Ctrl+l picks I-Log by hand). Under water, also turn the camera's stabilization off; on DJI, keep the Wide field of view.

  2. Double-click a clip

    It plays in color, steady, with your highlights on the timeline.

  3. Share or keep a moment

    Press l at the start and the end, then Shift+E to share it or k to keep it.

Clips you shot in the normal mode play as they always did, with your highlights, moments and one-key sharing.

Does it work?

Built on dive trips, on proven open-source tools.

Action Footage Player is used every day with a DJI Osmo Action 6 by the diver who made it, and is checked on a GoPro HERO12 Black. Its stabilization comes from Gyroflow's open-source engine. Under water it also corrects for how water bends light: on a test clip, that left about eight times less jitter than stabilizing as on land.

Do I need an HDR screen?

No. Log shows its best on HDR screens: recent iPhones, MacBook Pros, HDR TVs and monitors. But on Apple devices it looks good on ordinary screens too, in the player and in the clips you export from it.

On some other phones, computers and TVs, an exported clip can look a little flatter than one recorded in the camera's normal mode. If that's where you mostly share, the player has looks made for ordinary screens, for watching and for exporting; for DJI's D-Log M, the camera maker's own is among them.

Do I have to change my camera settings?

No, but one setting gets you the most. Choose log as the color setting once: D-Log M on DJI, GP-Log on GoPro, 10-bit I-Log on Insta360. I-Log isn't recognized by itself: in the player, Ctrl+l picks it by hand. Under water, also turn the camera's stabilization off: the camera then records how it moves, and the player steadies the clip from that. DJI cameras record it only with EIS off and the Wide field of view.

Clips you shot in the normal mode play as they always did, with your highlights, moments and one-key sharing.

The catch with log: other apps show those clips flat and grey. So you watch them here, or share a clip you exported from here.

Does it change my original files?

Only if you ask. To cut a spoiled part out of a clip (Ctrl+x), the player rewrites the clip without it, with nothing re-encoded, and the original goes to the Trash. Otherwise your originals stay as they are.

What it makes goes into the trip's project folder, which you pick once in the side panel's Project tab: _Moments for the parts you keep, _Clips for a preview of each clip, _Exports for the videos you export, and _ActionFootagePlayer for its own notes. Your own marks and a clip's Gyroflow project are small files beside the clip. On a locked card or a folder it can't write to, the project folder goes in Documents instead.

Is it really free?

Yes. It's open source under GPL-3.0, and the code is on GitHub.

Nothing is locked, and there is no account to make.

Which computers does it run on?

Mac: Apple Silicon with macOS 15 or later. Windows: 10 or 11, on 64-bit Intel or AMD, or on ARM. The Mac app is the one in daily use. The Windows app works too, but it's an alpha with little testing so far: the Intel and AMD version has been tried on a PC, the ARM version only by the automated builds.

Neither app is signed by a developer account yet. On a Mac, allow it once in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway. On Windows, SmartScreen shows a warning on first launch: More info, Run anyway.

What does an exported file look like?

An HDR video file that iPhones, Macs, most HDR TVs and YouTube show as HDR. Exported on a Mac in the HLG+DV look, the one clips start in on a Mac screen without HDR, it also carries Dolby Vision information, which iPhones, iPads and Macs use to show it well on screens without HDR. Exported in NBCU's or the camera maker's SDR look, it's a standard SDR file (Rec.709) instead.

The file has no look added, so it is a natural starting point: adjust it to your taste in an editor that handles HDR, such as iMovie. A look that is baked into a file can't be taken out again: shadows pushed to black stay black.

Why not just use the camera maker's app?

Use it if you like its look. Phone apps are great for quick edits, and some can now show and export log as HDR. That HDR tends to be styled for social media: exposure that shifts as the scene changes, brighter mid-tones, strong local contrast, deep shadows pushed to black.

Action Footage Player does the opposite: it converts log directly, keeps the scene's light as it was and adds no look of its own, so the clip looks the way the scene did. You can always add a look later; you can't take one out.

It also runs on your computer, plays your whole archive as it is, works with footage from several camera makers, and steadies clips while they play.

Who needs the stabilization in the player?

If you shoot above water with the camera's stabilization on, you probably don't: it does a good job in air.

It's for clips shot with the camera's stabilization off. Under water, where the camera's own fails, that is the way to shoot. It also covers clips where you turned it off for the widest view, and archives full of such footage. The camera still records how it moves (on DJI, only with EIS off and the Wide field of view), and Action Footage Player uses that while the clip plays.

A clip shot with the camera's stabilization on, HyperSmooth included, plays as it was recorded: stabilizing it twice would make it worse. To steady one anyway, make a Gyroflow project for it (Ctrl+e opens it in Gyroflow), and the player plays it with that.
