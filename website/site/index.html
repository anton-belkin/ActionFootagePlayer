Stabilization

Steady, even under water.

Under water the camera’s own stabilization gives up. Turn it off, and the camera records how it moves: the player steadies the clip from that as it plays, and corrects for how water bends light.

Built on Gyroflow’s open-source engine. Measured on a test clip: 8× less jitter than stabilizing as on land.

Common questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The player is open source under GPL-3.0, and the code is on GitHub. Nothing in it is locked, and there is no account to make.

Do I have to change my camera settings?

No, but one setting gets you the most. Choose log as the color setting once: D-Log M on DJI, GP-Log on GoPro, 10-bit I-Log on Insta360. Under water, also turn the camera's stabilization off: the camera then records how it moves, and the player steadies the clip from that.

Clips you shot in the normal mode play as they always did, with your highlights, moments and one-key sharing.

Do I need an HDR screen?

No. Log shows its best on HDR screens, but on Apple devices it looks good on ordinary screens too, in the player and in the clips you export from it. For other screens the player has looks made for them; for DJI's D-Log M, the camera maker's own is among them.

Does it change my original files?

Only if you ask. To cut a spoiled part out of a clip (Ctrl+x), the player rewrites the clip without it, with nothing re-encoded, and the original goes to the Trash. Otherwise your originals stay as they are.

Why not just use the camera maker's app?

Use it if you like its look. Phone apps are great for quick edits. Action Footage Player converts log directly, keeps the scene's light as it was and adds no look of its own, so the clip looks the way the scene did. You can always add a look later; you can't take one out.

It also runs on your computer, plays your whole archive as it is, works with footage from several camera makers, and steadies clips while they play.

Who needs the stabilization in the player?

Above water, with the camera's stabilization on, you probably don't: it does a good job in air. It's for clips shot with it off. Under water, where the camera's own fails, that is the way to shoot.

How do I install it?

Download, open, drag to Applications. The apps aren't signed by a developer account yet: on a Mac, allow it once in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway; on Windows, choose More info, then Run anyway. Downloads ›
