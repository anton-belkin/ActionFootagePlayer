Organizing

Your footage as a project of its own, wherever it lives.

Keep a trip's best parts as moments, mark what matters, and find any clip by camera, place or tag, on this computer or your NAS. Action Footage Library, the app that comes with the player, sees the whole of it, and works through it in the background while you watch.

Download free for MacDownload for Mac Apple Silicon Download for WindowsDownload free for Windows 10 / 11 · alpha
  • Nothing to import. Your footage stays in its folders. Moments, previews, photos and exports are files beside it, which FinderFile Explorer shows like any other.
  • It moves with the footage. Every file knows its clip by what the clip is, not only by where it was. Move, copy or rename folders, and everything stays linked.
  • Viewing is never saving. Opening and watching a clip writes nothing to it. The player writes only when you press a key or a button for it.

A project

Where your footage is, and where what you make goes.

A project says two things. Where your original clips are: any folders, on this computer or a NAS. And where what you make from them goes: moments in _Moments, clip previews in _Clips, exports in _Exports, photos in _Stills.

That's all its file, Project.afproject, keeps: the folders the footage is in, called its locations. Everything else is in the files themselves. A moment carries its clip's identity inside it, and so does a photo; a clip's marks and saved view are in a small file beside the clip. So the project moves with the footage: copy the whole folder, footage and project together, to another disk or a NAS, and everything works as it did.

One project per trip, or one for all your footage: both work. The originals are never changed, and they can stay where the camera's Wi-Fi upload or your NAS put them.

Starting one

The player never makes a project on its own. The first time you save a moment or export, its side panel opens on the Project tab and says No project yet. Pick the folder from the list, which goes from the clip's own folder up to the top of the disk, and press Initialize the project here. Every folder inside it is part of the project, and more locations can be added in the Library. Double-click Project.afproject to open it in Action Footage Library.

The project's own files can live apart from the footage, on a fast SSD say, with the clips on the NAS's big disks: the Library's Move the project takes Project.afproject and the project's folders there, and the old folder becomes a location.

Marks need no project: they're kept beside the clip anyway.

The files

Everything is a file you can see.

A project after a few trips: its own folders on the SSD, the footage on the NAS. The project's folders start with an underscore, so they sort first.

The sizes on the right tell the story: the originals and the exports are big, the moments and the clips' flip-books are tiny. Keep hundreds of them, and copy them anywhere.

What the player and the Library make lands in a Generated folder inside its kind's folder. From there, move it anywhere in the project, rename it, make folders of your own: it stays found, because each file carries its link inside. Delete a moment's file, and the moment is gone.

Beside the project file is .afp, the shared catalog: what the Library knows of the files, so it opens fast. It can be deleted: it's built again from the files.

Action Footage Library

The whole project, in an app of its own.

What you do to one clip happens in the player. What you do to many happens in Action Footage Library, the app that comes with the player. It browses the project as folders, finds clips by what they are, and runs what you ask in the background, one task after another, while you go on watching.

Browse

Five views across the top: Originals, the locations as they are on disk; Footage, the clips as you arranged their previews in _Clips; Moments, Photos and Exports. Folders open with a double-click, as in FinderFile Explorer, in a grid of pictures or a list. A clip opens in the player.

Filters and search

Filters narrow the folder you're in: by camera, by color (log, HDR or SDR), by whether a clip has motion data or is 360°, by marks and moments, tags, renders, and where it was recorded, each with how many match. Type in the search field to list every match below, or in the whole view.

Commands on what you select

Select as in FinderFile Explorer: a click, ⌘Ctrl+click, Shift+click, a folder for everything in it. The Commands on the right act on that selection and nothing else: clip previews, moments and photos of the marks, renders, exports, Stabilize optically, checks, tags, the location.

Tasks and Info

Tasks lists what runs and waits, each with how far it is, what it's on and Stop; what couldn't be made is listed with why. While they run, the Mac doesn't go to sleep. Info tells what you selected: a clip's date, length, camera, lens profile and place, or a folder summed up.

To open it: Open in Action Footage Library in the player's Project tab, or double-click Project.afproject. It makes nothing with code of its own: each task is the player run without a window, so everything comes out exactly as the player makes it.

On a Mac it's beside the player in the downloadWindows it's installed with the player, with its own Start menu entry. On Linux, the player does one clip at a time for now, and the Library is on its way.

Locations

Footage on several disks, one project.

The camera uploads to the NAS's fast inbox, the clips move to the archive later, the ones you edit get a copy on your SSD. The project sees them as one tree of folders.

Locations

In the project's settings, Locations lists the folders the footage is in, each placed in the project's tree: at its top, or as a folder of it (the inbox as Action 6/, say). They're written as network paths, so every computer finds them. A clip is taken from the first location that has it, so a copy on your SSD plays in place of the archive's.

Found from FinderFile Explorer

Saving the locations leaves a small file at the top of each, <project>.afplocation, so a clip you double-click finds its project, its moments and its marks. A location taken out loses its file; nothing else in it changes.

Take in another project

A trip made on a laptop, copied to the NAS? Take in another project… brings its moments, previews, photos and exports into this one, once, and adds its locations where its footage isn't already inside this project. Its Project.afproject then goes to the TrashRecycle Bin. It lists what it will do before it starts.

Checks

Check on a selection tells what's wrong: an original missing, a moment whose clip isn't found, the same clip twice, a clip with two own files. Find moved files looks for the missing ones across the project's locations and mends their links, asking where it can't tell.

Links

Links that follow the files.

A moment, a clip preview, a photo and an export each know their clip by what the clip is: its size, the time the camera wrote inside it, and a fingerprint of its content. Where it was is only a hint. So footage moved to another folder or location, or renamed, stays linked: the link finds it again and notes its new place.

Your own files are found the same way: by the link inside them, in any folder of the project. Organize moments and previews as you like.

In the project's settings, I never rename my footage lets a moved clip be found by its name too, which is quicker on a big archive.

The catalog

A shared catalog, so it opens fast.

The Library keeps what it knows of the project's files next to the project, in .afp. A project of thousands of clips on a NAS opens in a moment, from any computer: a computer that opens it for the first time reads the catalog instead of looking at every file.

Whatever the player and the Library do shows in the view at once: a moment saved, a task's files, a tag. Changes made in FinderFile Explorer show when the Library next looks at the folders, within minutes. The files stay the truth: the catalog is only what was last seen of them.

Where and how

Where each clip was recorded, and with which lens.

The place, from your photos

An action camera rarely records where it was. Your phone did: select clips and press Suggest from Apple Photos in the Library's Location, and the photos with a place you took nearest in time suggest it. Each suggestion shows how far apart in time they were; you keep the ones that fit. Photos is only read, never changed; the Mac asks you once to let the Library read it. A clip whose camera recorded GPS has its place already. Or set it by hand: latitude and longitude as a map copies them, and a dive site's name if you like.

The place is written into each original's own file, beside it, so it travels with the clip. Info shows the place's name on a small map, and the Location filter finds clips by country, region and place.

The lens profile in Info

Stabilization needs the lens's profile. Info on a clip says which one the player uses: the one the camera wrote in the file, or the best-rated profile for that camera and mode in Gyroflow's database. Where none fits yet, it shows the camera's mode number, which is what to tell us to get one added.

  • Suggestions from Apple Photos are on a Mac. On Windows, set the place by hand.
  • Grouped by the clip's clock. Cameras keep time differently: DJI, Insta360 and recent GoPros in universal time, older GoPros in local time. The Library reads each camera's way, so the photos it compares are from the right hour.

Pictures

A picture for every clip, as fast as FinderFile Explorer draws one.

The Library asks the system for its own thumbnails, Quick Look's on a MacFile Explorer's on Windows: 300 clips on a NAS show in 9 seconds the first time, and in under a second after. Then, for the whole trip in color, there are clip previews.

Make clip previews

Log footage (a recording mode that keeps more of the light, and looks flat and grey until it's converted) is a wall of grey icons. Select clips or folders, and Make clip previews makes one small MP4 for each clip without one: a flip-book of the clip's marked frames, or of about 12 frames spread through it, in color. One at a time, in the background.

Laid out as you like

They land in _Clips/Generated: as the footage's folders, by trips (clips at most 2 days apart together), or all in one folder. In Finder's icon view it's the trip in color, and Quick Look (the space bar) flips through a clip in seconds.In large icons it's the trip in color. Open a preview in the player, and the whole clip plays.

Sort them

They're files: copy them, sort them into folders of your own, rename them as you like. Each one stays a link to its original clip, and the Library's Footage view shows the clips as you arranged them.

Set a clip aside

In the player, X moves the clip's preview to the TrashRecycle Bin: "Not interesting". The clip itself stays. X again makes the preview again; for a clip with none, the Project tab's Make this clip's preview makes just that one.

Stabilize optically

No motion data in a clip? No problem.

The player steadies a clip from the motion the camera recorded with it. A DJI camera records it only with its stabilization (EIS) off, and some cameras record none. For those, Gyroflow finds the motion in the picture itself.

Select the clips, or their folders, and press Stabilize optically. Gyroflow measures each clip's motion from its picture, one clip after another in the background, in about the time the clip plays (half as long again at 4K 60 fps). Its project goes beside each clip, and from then on the player plays it steady, like any other.

Lens chooses the lens for the clips: each clip's own, as the camera recorded, or a type where the camera has several (an Osmo Action 6: Wide or Normal). It's kept with each clip, and the player uses it too. A lens chosen, or Redo, makes the clips' Gyroflow projects again, their settings kept.

Clips that don't need it are left out and listed with why: those with motion data the player uses, with a Gyroflow project already, 360° clips, and clips whose lens isn't known yet.

It uses Gyroflow's optical analysis from the command line, which is coming to Gyroflow's own releases. Until then it needs Gyroflow built from our fork. One clip at a time, in the player, works with any Gyroflow: Ctrl+e, see Stabilization.

Tags and renders

Words for finding things, and moments made ahead.

Tags

Put any words on clips, moments, previews, photos and exports: dolphin, for family, best. Tags in the Commands sets and removes them on the selection; the filters and the search find them. A tag is kept with its item: in the clip's own file, in the link inside a moment or preview, and in a photo's metadata, where Apple Photos and Lightroom read it as a keyword.

Renders

In Moments, Render makes the selected moments as finished videos, in HDR, SDR or both, in the background, into _Renders. Each render knows what it was made from: change the moment, and it shows as stale; Render again makes only what changed. An export in the same look copies a current render instead of rendering again. Deleting renders breaks nothing.

Moments and photos of all the marks

On a selection of clips: Moments of the marks makes a moment of each mark that isn't in one yet, 2 seconds on each side, named by when it happened. Photos of the marks saves a photo at each one, as stills, Live Photos or Motion Photos.

Export

In Moments, Export makes a video of each selected moment, each with its own settings, as Shift+E exports one: in HDR as the player shows it, or in SDR. One already exported in that look is skipped.

In the player: marks

The camera's highlights, then your own marks.

A mark is a point in a clip. Pressed the highlight button while recording? Those marks are already on the navigation bar under the video, as amber diamonds: the quick switch button or remote on a DJI Osmo Action 4, Action 5 Pro or Action 6, HiLight on a GoPro HERO5 or later. Yours are white. Point at one, and the bar says its name and time to the hundredth; a click goes to its exact frame. Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[, or the bar's arrows, jump to the next and the previous one.

M adds a mark where the video is. On a mark (within half a second), M removes it, the camera's too.

The first change writes <clip>.afp.json beside the clip, with the camera's marks copied into it. From then on, that file is the clip's marks. It's also where the clip's saved view goes: what you keep with Save to the source in the side panel's Source and Transform tabs, so the clip opens as you saved it. Delete the file, and the camera's highlights are back as they were, and the saved view is forgotten. The clip itself is never written to.

Marks need no project. In the side panel, Marks of this clip lists them: "DJI quick switch" or "GoPro HiLight" for the camera's, "Mark 3" for yours. Click one to jump there.

In the player: moments

A moment is a small file. Open it, and the original plays.

Press l at the start of a part, l at its end, then k, and type a name.

The name

The player asks "Moment 0:20–0:30 name (Enter: Turtle 2):". Enter alone takes the suggestion: your last name with the next number, or, before you've typed any, the date and time it was filmed. Tab puts the suggestion in the field to edit, ↑ brings back earlier names, Esc saves nothing.

The file

It becomes Turtle.afplink.mp4 in _Moments/Generated: a small MP4 that shows one color frame of the part (the first mark in it, else its middle), with a link to the original inside. Finder and Quick LookFile Explorer show that frame. Its file dates are when it was filmed, so sorting by date puts moments in the order they happened.

Playing it

Opened in the player, it plays its part of the original, in full quality, then the next moment in the folder. From a NAS it shows in seconds: a moment of a 2.5 GB clip in 3 to 5 seconds, since the player reads the motion data of the part it plays first and the rest while it plays. Open a folder of moments itself, and all of them play in the order they happened. A double-click does this when Action Footage Player is the app that opens MP4 files (Get Info → Open with → Change All…); otherwise the system plays the one-frame preview.

Deleting the file deletes the moment

There's no list to keep in step. Rename it, move it into a folder of your own, tag it: it stays a moment. In the side panel, Moments of this clip has Rename… and Remove, which moves it to the TrashRecycle Bin.

Changing its ends

Play the moment, move its start or end with l, then k. The player asks "Update Turtle to …? Enter, or a new name for a new one". Its Finder tags stay.

Viewed as it was when you saved it

A moment is made whole, from what plays when you press k: its stabilization, level, horizon, lens, color aberration correction, temporal denoise and frame. If the clip has a Gyroflow project, the moment gets its own copy, trimmed to the part. A moment never changes by itself: changing the clip, or the clip's project, later doesn't reach it. How the clip is read (its log curve, its turn, the lens under water) stays the clip's, for all its moments.

While a moment plays, the side panel's Transform tab says Transform defined in the moment. Change anything, and it's for viewing only, unless Auto save is on: "Changed for viewing. The dot marks what's saved". Save to the moment writes it, Revert to saved goes back. Switch Settings of its own off, and the moment plays as its clip is saved to play; its own settings and its Gyroflow copy go, the copy to the TrashRecycle Bin.

In the player: exports

Exports go into _Exports, with a link back.

Shift+E exports the marked part, or the whole clip, as it plays: steady and in color. It asks for a name in the same way as a moment, suggesting the moment's name when the part is one: "Export 0:20–0:30 (SDR) as (Enter: Turtle):". Shift+E again while it runs cancels it.

The file is _Exports/Turtle.mov. A plain name is HDR (bright highlights on screens that can show them); other looks say so: Turtle (SDR).mov, Turtle (Dolby Vision).mov. Each export carries a link to its original. A part that wasn't a moment yet is also saved as one, export_Turtle, so you can delete exports to make room and still play the part in full quality later.

The side panel's Output tab has the same, under Export: Export the part or Export the clip, Export in SDR, Show the last export. To export many moments in one go, use Action Footage Library. The looks and formats are on the Log footage page.

In the player: cutting out

Cut out the minute filmed in your pocket.

Mark the spoiled part with l and l, press Ctrl+x, and confirm. The clip is rewritten without that part, without re-encoding: every frame kept is copied exactly. It needs no project.

The player asks first: "Cut 0:05–0:20 out of DJI_20261024094541_0093_D.MP4? Kept: 0:00–0:05 and 0:20–end (two clips; the second from the keyframe at or before 0:20), without re-encoding. The original goes to the Trash." Cut it out or Keep the clip as it is.

What it keeps

  • A part in the middle leaves two clips. The first keeps the original's name. The second is named by its own recording time, like DJI_20261024094600_0093_D.MP4.
  • The second clip starts at the keyframe (a full frame the others are built from) at or before the cut's end, so up to about a second of the spoiled part can stay.
  • Less than half a second left on a side is dropped.
  • The camera's data stays: lens, color mode, motion data for stabilization, timecode, sound. Each new clip is read back and checked before anything is replaced; if it doesn't match, nothing changes.
  • Each new clip gets the original's saved view, and the marks and moments that are in it. A moment entirely inside the cut-out part goes to the TrashRecycle Bin.

What it changes

  • The original goes to the TrashRecycle Bin, with its side files: the .LRF, its Gyroflow project, its .afp.json and its clip preview. On a network share or a memory card, which have no Recycle Bin, they go into the project's .Trash folder instead, and the message says so.
  • There's no undo in the player. You can take the original back out of the TrashRecycle Bin by hand, but the first new clip now has its name.
  • Only DJI clips are supported for now. We're working on the others. Until then, a GoPro clip is cut only if it passes a check first, and Insta360 and iPhone clips are refused.
  • Exports aren't touched, and their links still point at the original. For clip previews of the new clips, use Make clip previews in Action Footage Library.

Into your editor

Your moments and marks, in Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro. Coming soon

When you've chosen, take the trip to the edit. The editor gets your original clips, at their own timecode, with your moments, marks, tags and folders on them. Nothing to find again, nothing to mark twice.

  1. Choose the editor

    In the Project tab, Marks for the editor, then Final Cut Pro…, DaVinci Resolve… or Premiere Pro…. By default every clip goes in except the ones you set aside with X; With moments or marks takes only the clips that have one.

  2. The player writes the file

    It goes in the project folder, named after the project: "Final Cut Pro: 14 clips, 23 moments, 41 marks → 2026-10 Egypt (Final Cut Pro).fcpxml", and Finder shows itits folder opens.

  3. Import it, once

    The editors take an import once and don't merge a second one. So do it when you've finished choosing. Writing it again replaces the file.

Limits

What to know before you rely on it.

  • A moment is a link. It needs its original somewhere in the project's locations, unchanged. Moved or renamed is fine; a re-encoded original is another file, and its moments lose it.
  • A moment file isn't for sending. Elsewhere it's one still frame, and messengers and re-encoding strip the link. Send an export.
  • A moment's file shows one frame, not a video of the part.
  • Clip previews aren't stabilized, and their frames are the nearest keyframes, up to about a second from the mark.
  • Marks have no names you can type yet: they're "Mark 1", "Mark 2", or the camera's.
  • A project needs a folder it can write to. A card with its lock on: copy it to your disk or NAS first, and make the project there.
  • Cutting replaces the clip. The original goes to the TrashRecycle Bin, there's no undo in the player, exports keep pointing at the old original, and it's made for DJI clips.
  • Apple Photos albums that follow the project's folders come in a later version; today the Library reads Photos for places only.
  • On Linux, the player does one clip at a time for now. Action Footage Library isn't on Linux yet, so there the tasks for many clips at once wait for it.
  • On Windows, projects, moments, previews and Action Footage Library have had little testing so far.

Questions

Before you start.

Does the player change my footage?

Only when you cut a part out with Ctrl+x and confirm: then the clip is replaced by the parts kept, and the original goes to the TrashRecycle Bin. Otherwise it only reads your clips. Beside each one it writes a small file of the clip's own, <clip>.afp.json, once you change a mark, save the clip's view, or give it a place or a tag, and a Gyroflow project when you make one. Everything else goes in the project's folders.

Do I need a project for marks?

No. Marks are kept beside the clip, project or not. Moments, clip previews, exports, X and Action Footage Library need one.

I moved my footage to the NAS. Are my moments lost?

No. Add the new folder to the project's locations, or move the whole project folder with it. A moment finds its clip by what the clip is, not only by where it was, and notes its new place. Check in the Library shows anything still missing, and Find moved files mends it.

One project for everything, or one per trip?

Either. One project for all your footage lets the Library search and filter across every trip; a project per trip is a folder you can move or archive whole. A trip's project can be taken into a bigger one later with Take in another project….

Can I send a moment to a friend?

Send an export instead: Shift+E on the moment. The moment's own file is a frame and a link to a clip only you have.

Does the Library change my Apple Photos?

No. It reads the time and place of your photos to suggest where clips were recorded, and changes nothing in Photos.
