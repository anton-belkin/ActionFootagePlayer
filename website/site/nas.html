NAS Helper

Your footage on the NAS, steady on every screen at home.

Keep your trips on a NAS, and a small container there does three things: it lets several people work in one library together, each seeing the others' changes in a second; it streams your clips and moments stabilized and in HDR to the TV, a phone or a browser; and, if you like, it puts them in Jellyfin, Emby or Kodi.

A capable NAS Docker Alpha

How it works

Three jobs, each one a switch.

It runs next to your footage, on the NAS's own processor and graphics. Each job can be turned on or off on its own.

Collaboration

Several people can work in one library at once: one marks moments on a laptop, another sorts the clips on a desktop, a third copies in a new trip. The NAS is told at once what changes on its own disks and writes it into the project's catalog, so everyone's player and Library shows the others' work about a second later, without anyone waiting for the share to be read again. It writes nothing but the catalog.

The stream

Every clip, moment and clip preview of the project becomes a video stream, the way the player shows it: stabilized with Gyroflow's engine on the NAS's graphics, D-Log M in HDR, each file with its own saved settings. It's rendered as you watch and kept for next time, and the frame never leaves the graphics chip.

Links for media servers

If you turn it on, your moments (or your clips, or both: its scope) appear in Jellyfin, Emby or Kodi, sorted into trips, with their titles, dates and covers. Their apps on the TV, the phone or the tablet then play the streams as they are, without converting them again.

What it needs

A capable NAS that runs Docker.

The NAS

An x86-64 NAS with some power and graphics that decode and encode HEVC in hardware (VA-API): Intel or AMD. Tried on a UGREEN DXP with an Intel N100, which renders a 1080p stream at 67-69 frames a second, more than a 60 fps clip needs.

Docker

It ships as a Docker image with an example compose file. You say which folders it sees: each project, the shares its footage is on, and two folders of its own.

A project

The trip as the Library makes it: a folder with its Project.afproject, on the NAS, with its footage on the NAS's shares.

Some space

What played stays in a cache, 40 GB by default, so a clip watched again starts at once.

Watching

Press play on the TV or the phone.

The helper's page, http://<your NAS>:8099, lists the clips by folder, each with its stream.

Fast to start

A clip never played starts in about 5 s, one whose opening was rendered ahead in about 2 s, one played before at once. One click renders the openings of a whole folder in the background, while nobody watches.

In HDR, or as the TV wants it

Streams are HEVC in HDR (HLG). Older TVs that take streams only in MPEG-TS get them that way, and a TV that shows HLG too dark can get HDR10, made brighter for a lit room.

Seek anywhere

Every stream lists all its parts from the start, so players start at the beginning and can jump ahead; the render follows.

Your settings

What you set in the player (horizon lock, the view, a moment's own settings) is what streams. Change a setting and the clip is rendered again.

Media servers

Jellyfin, Emby, Kodi, and every app that plays from them.

The links are small files that media servers have read for years: a .strm with the stream's address and a .nfo with the title, date and length, beside a cover.

Scope

Moments, clips, or both.

AFP_JELLYFIN_SCOPE says what goes to the media server. What's outside it is taken out of the folder, so you can change your mind any time.

moments

The moments you marked, each in its trip: the short, best parts. A good start for a family library.

clips

Each clip preview: the whole clip, as you sorted them into trips in the Library.

all

Both, in two folders.

Settings

Technical, and every setting described.

Everything is set in the container's environment. --help describes each setting, and the helper's /settings page shows them all with their values and how each job is doing.

AFP_COLLABORATION · AFP_STREAM · AFP_JELLYFINEach job on or off. The media server links are off until you turn them on.
AFP_JELLYFIN_SCOPEmoments, clips or all.
AFP_BITRATE · AFP_HEIGHTThe streams' quality: 30 Mbit/s and 1080p by default.
AFP_TV_LOOKWhat older TVs get: HLG, HDR10 or SDR, brighter, at 30 fps.
AFP_CACHE_GBHow much of what played stays on the NAS.
AFP_PUBLIC_URLThe NAS's address, as Jellyfin's apps reach it.

Heavy clips

Rendered ahead, played smoothly.

Some clips ask more than a NAS can render while you watch: 8K, high frame rates, a long dive. Render them ahead, whole, and they play from the NAS like any video file.

A folder at a time

One click on the helper's page renders a folder's clips whole, in the background, one at a time, while nobody watches. What's rendered stays in the cache.

Smooth, however heavy

Played from what was rendered ahead, an 8K or 120 fps clip plays as smoothly as any other, on any screen.

And again when you change it

Change a clip's settings in the player and it's rendered again, ahead, the same way.

Limits

Good to know.

  • It's an alpha. It's been tried on one NAS so far: a UGREEN DXP with an Intel N100.
  • Graphics with hardware video. The streams are rendered with VA-API, which Intel and AMD graphics have; NVIDIA's cards don't offer it for encoding.
  • For the home network. There's no login: keep it out of reach from the internet, or put a VPN in front.
  • D-Log M in HDR. Other log curves stream as recorded for now.
  • 8K clips render a little slower than they play on an N100 (about 42 frames a second for 50): players buffer a bit more.
  • More than one stream at once may be too much. A NAS like the N100 renders one 4K60 clip only a little faster than it plays, so a second stream at the same time can stutter. Clips rendered ahead play any number at once.
  • Dolby Vision isn't in the streams yet: they're HLG, or HDR10 for TVs.
