Opening clips

No import, no library, nothing to render first: the clip plays where it is.

Double-click a clip

The Mac app's setup makes it the default app for MP4 videos (macOS asks you to confirm): double-clicking one plays it with everything on this page. Other videos open with it from Finder's Open With.

Finder's Quick Actions

macOS

Right click → Quick Actions: Play Stabilized opens the selected videos in the player; Stabilize + LUT exports them in the background, with a notification as each one is done.

Explorer's menu

Windows

ActionFootagePlayer is in Open With for MP4 and MOV videos, and Explorer's menu has Play in ActionFootagePlayer and Stabilize + LUT, which exports in a window of its own that shows how it goes (on Windows 11, under Show more options).

One window, the whole folder

panel → Source, Output

A video opened while one plays takes its place. The other videos in the folder are the next and previous clips: In the folder, at the bottom of the side panel's Source and Output, has Previous clip and Next clip. With Autoplay next on (the default), the next clip in the folder plays when one ends; off, each clip pauses at its end. Loop the clip plays it again and again.

What the clip is

panel → Source

At the top of the side panel's Source: the camera, how it recorded (log and its curve, HDR, or SDR, and the Dolby Vision profile of an HDR file), its lens, whether it has motion data and telemetry, and how many marks it has.

How to read it, set once

panel → Source

Three things say how the file itself is to be read: its Log curve, whether it was Recorded turned, and whether its lens was Under water, flat port. They're found from the file and the camera; change one, and it's for viewing until Save to the source keeps it for the clip and all its moments and clip previews. Revert to saved goes back. Each clip keeps its own: nothing of this carries over to the next clip.

Originals stay as they are

Your originals stay as they are, unless you cut a spoiled part out: then the clip is rewritten without it, nothing re-encoded, and the original goes to the Trash. Beside the clip go only small files of yours: its Gyroflow project, a .gyroflow file, and <clip>.afp.json, with your marks and how you saved the clip to play. Moments and exports go to the project folder.

Straight from the camera

Play clips as they come off the card. An app that exports or trims them drops the metadata the player reads: the log mode, the motion data, the telemetry, the highlights.

Log in color & looks

Log is the camera's mode that records the most, but its clips come out flat and grey and normally need grading. Here they play in full color straight away.

Log footage, explained step by step →

Log recognized by itself

The clip's metadata says it's log, not its file name, so there's nothing to set up: D-Log M on the Osmo Action 6 and Osmo 360, GP-Log and GP-Log2 on recent GoPros. Normal clips play as usual.

Verified Osmo Action 6 (D-Log M); GoPro HERO12 Black (GP-Log, firmware 1.20 or later) Should work Osmo 360; HERO13 Black; GP-Log2 (MISSION 1)

Log in HDR

Ctrl+h

D-Log M, D-Log, GP-Log, GP-Log2 and 10-bit I-Log are converted to HDR (Rec.2100 HLG): a fixed technical transform, the same for every clip, with no creative look added. Ctrl+h goes straight to HDR.

The log curve by hand

Ctrl+l · panel → Source

For clips whose metadata doesn't say: D-Log M, D-Log, GP-Log, GP-Log2 and I-Log (10-bit), in the side panel's Source → Log curve, or in turn with Ctrl+l. It's how the clip is read: Save to the source keeps it, for the clip and all its moments and clip previews. A file tagged HDR isn't taken for log; Grade as log by hand… is there if it is log after all.

Should work Insta360 single-lens, 10-bit I-Log Not yet 8-bit I-Log

The SDR looks

Ctrl+s

SDR through NBCUniversal's neutral HLG → SDR conversion (the same for every camera), through the camera maker's own Rec.709 LUT (DJI's, for D-Log M), or through mpv's tone mapping. Ctrl+s goes through them with the HDR outputs.

Not yet a maker's Rec.709 LUT for the curves other than D-Log M

DJI's LUT, from DJI

DJI's Rec.709 LUT isn't redistributed: the app's setup downloads it from DJI and checks it. HDR and NBCU's SDR work without it.

As recorded

L

Log handling off for this clip: the file as it is, flat as the camera recorded it.

HDR files play as they are

A file tagged HDR, in HLG or PQ (an export, a phone's or an action camera's HDR video), plays in HDR with no LUT, and is never taken for log and graded a second time. Ctrl+s shows it in SDR, through NBCU's conversion or mpv's tone mapping. In the Mac app it also gets the Dolby Vision looks, with the player's trims: HLG+DV for an HLG file, PQ+DV for a PQ one. A file with Dolby Vision of its own (profile 8) also plays with it: HDR, its own Dolby Vision and SDR, its own Dolby Vision. Profile 5 has no HDR base to build on, so mpv tones it down.

Should work the PQ+DV looks

Looks by what the clip is

panel → Output → Display

The side panel's Output → Display lists only the looks this clip has, under a line that says what it is, for example From Log, D-Log M:

  • log: HDR, the two HLG+DV looks (Mac app), the three SDR looks, and As recorded, log;
  • HDR, HLG or PQ: as recorded, the Dolby Vision looks (Mac app), SDR by mpv's tone mapping and SDR by NBCU's conversion;
  • SDR: one look, SDR, as recorded. If it's log in fact, pick its curve in Source.

The look belongs to the screen, not to the clip: it isn't saved with the clip.

HDR on any screen

On an HDR screen, log is a technical conversion, the same for every clip, so it's done automatically. On a Mac without one, macOS tones the HDR down for that screen, with each frame's own light levels.

HDR, SDR and Dolby Vision, explained step by step →

What clips start in

On an HDR screen (a MacBook Pro's, for one), log clips start in HDR. On a Mac without an HDR screen (a MacBook Air, most external monitors), the Mac app starts them in HDR, HLG+DV: HLG shown through macOS's own Dolby Vision® display management, which tones it down for that screen, the way QuickTime and Apple's devices show the export. What you choose with Ctrl+s is what the next clips start in on that kind of screen, for the session.

The video outputs

Ctrl+s

The clip's next video output, of the ones it has:

  • HDR, HLG: HDR; on a screen without HDR, macOS tones it down;
  • HDR, HLG+DV: HDR on an HDR screen, toned down by the trims on one without;
  • SDR, HLG+DV: toned down by the trims on an HDR screen too: what an SDR screen shows;
  • SDR through mpv's tone mapping, through NBCUniversal's neutral HLG → SDR conversion, or through the camera maker's own Rec.709 LUT (DJI's, for D-Log M).

On an HDR screen, the SDR ones show the way an SDR screen would show them. A file already in PQ HDR has the same two Dolby Vision outputs, as HDR, PQ+DV and SDR, PQ+DV.

Mac app the HLG+DV and PQ+DV outputs

Each frame's own light levels

Mac app

With HLG+DV, each frame's darkest, average and brightest levels go along with it (Dolby Vision's L1), measured on the GPU as it plays, so macOS maps every scene for what's in it rather than for a typical one: a dark dive and a sunlit beach each get their own room. Exports made in an HLG+DV look measure each of their frames the same way.

Trims: the author's, or neutral

Mac app

The trims go on top of the frame's levels: the author's, to taste, fitted to NBCUniversal's HLG → SDR conversion, with no scene-level offsets, so an HDR screen still gets HDR. The side panel's Dolby Vision has them as sliders, with a way back to the author's and to neutral ones (Dolby Vision's own mapping, nothing added). Exports made in an HLG+DV look take what's set there.

Exports for Apple devices

Mac app

Exported in an HLG+DV look (the one the Mac app starts in on a screen without HDR), a clip is HEVC Main10 HLG with Dolby Vision profile 8.4 metadata: the player writes compatible metadata (RPU) itself. Apple devices, an iPhone or a Mac, play it in HDR and on a standard screen apply the trims, so they show it as the player does. Devices without Dolby Vision play plain HLG and tone it down themselves.

A PQ file exported in a PQ+DV look gets Dolby Vision profile 8.1 the same way, on its HDR10 (PQ) base. A file's own Dolby Vision isn't carried into an export: without one of these looks, it exports as plain HDR.

Exports in HDR, HLG, and those from Stabilize + LUT and the command line, are plain HLG. Exports made on Windows or Linux never carry Dolby Vision.

Screens of the other kind

A clip keeps its look when its window goes to a screen of the other kind. On Windows and Linux every screen counts as HDR.

A monitor shown too dark

macOS

After an HDR display (an HDR TV, say) has been connected, macOS can show HDR about a stop darker on the SDR monitor plugged in where it was, until the Mac restarts. The player tells you when it sees that, the first time HDR plays on such a monitor.

Stabilization

With Gyroflow's engine, while the clip plays and at the same time as the log conversion: nothing is rendered first.

Stabilization, explained step by step →

From the camera's own motion data

Ctrl+g

Clips recorded with the camera's stabilization off play stabilized without a project: DJI with EIS off and the Wide FOV (only then does it record its motion), GoPro with HyperSmooth off, Insta360 with FlowState off. Only when the camera recorded that it was off: a clip the camera stabilized already plays as it is.

Verified Osmo Action 6 (EIS off); GoPro HERO12 Black (HyperSmooth off) Should work Osmo 360; other DJI cameras; other GoPros; Insta360 single-lens Not yet other cameras, without a project

Nothing to render first

Old trips play stabilized as they are, with no rendered copy of each clip to make and keep: a file is written only for the parts you export.

The stabilization itself is Gyroflow's engine; the player adds playing it in real time, HDR from log and the telemetry.

On, wider, off

Ctrl+g · panel → Transform

Transform → Stabilization: On zooms in so no edge shows; Wider doesn't zoom, so the stabilized picture covers about as much of the window as the frame, and the edges show at times, filled; Off isn't stabilized, and the lens is still corrected unless Lens is As recorded. Ctrl+g goes On → Wider → Off. A clip with a project starts On, as Gyroflow has it.

How strong

Ctrl+k

Transform → Level, for a clip without a project: Weak, Medium (the default) or Strong, 10, 20 or 30 % cropped, smoothed at 50, 75 or 100 %. It changes while the clip plays.

Horizon

panel → Transform

Level or Free, for a clip without a project. The default is the preset's: Free, with the one that comes with the player. Level needs more zoom, so the edges show more.

Saved with the clip

panel → Transform

How a clip is shown is its transform: stabilization, level, horizon, lens and projection, frame, and the color-fringe correction under water. It's kept as a whole, never one setting alone. The card at the top of Transform says where the one playing comes from: this session (nothing saved for it), the source, the moment or the clip preview.

  • Save (Save to the source, to the moment, to the clip preview) writes what plays there; for a clip, into <clip>.afp.json beside it. Each setting shows the saved choice with a dot in its corner.
  • Revert to saved goes back to what's saved.
  • Auto save (off by default, for this session) saves as you go: a clip with nothing saved gets what plays as it opens, then every change.
  • Use as default makes it what clips with nothing saved start with.

A clip with a saved transform opens as saved, whatever the defaults, and what you change on it is for viewing until you save it. On a clip with nothing saved, what you change sticks for the next clips too.

The lens, apart

panel → Transform → Lens

Corrected (the default) or As recorded, apart from the stabilization: with stabilization Off and the lens Corrected, only the lens is corrected and nothing is smoothed. A clip with a lens profile but no motion data plays that way. It needs the lens to be known (Source shows its profile); picking one by hand comes later.

Fisheye looks

panel → Transform → Lens

With the lens corrected, how it's laid flat: Linear, straight lines straight (the default), or Fisheye 1 to Fisheye 4 (stereographic, equidistant, equisolid, orthographic).

A frame for the whole clip

panel → Transform → Frame

The clip's shape: Own (the video's), 16:9, 4:3, 5:4 or 1:1, and As is, Landscape or Portrait, which makes 9:16, 3:4 or 4:5 of them. It plays that way, and exports, moments, clip previews and photos are made in it. Make a Gyroflow project… (Ctrl+e) writes it into a new project, where the zoom can be set too.

Clips shot turned, upright

panel → Source

A clip shot with the camera turned (upside down, or held vertical) plays, stabilizes and exports upright, as the file says. When a file doesn't say, Source → Recorded turned sets it: As is, 90° ↻, 180° or 90° ↺, kept with Save to the source. The turn comes before the stabilization.

Verified turned clips playing upright

Gyroflow projects

A project made in Gyroflow and saved next to the clip is used as it is: the clip plays as the project says. What it sets is the project's alone: the smoothing, the horizon, the frame, the projection, and how the clip was turned and whether its lens was under water. Transform lists them under In the Gyroflow project, with Edit in Gyroflow…; the player never writes them. Over a project you can look at it Wider or Off, or with the lens As recorded, for viewing only. A key for something the project sets says so, and that Ctrl+e edits it in Gyroflow.

Verified Osmo Action 6 Should work the other cameras, and any camera Gyroflow supports

Open in Gyroflow

Ctrl+e

Pauses and opens the clip's project in Gyroflow, written next to the clip first when it has none, from what you saw: the same lens, smoothing, zoom, frame and refraction. Each time you save there, the stabilization here reloads at the same spot. A project that's already there is never overwritten.

Windows a clip without a project needs gyroflow_app set in gyroflow.confLinux gyroflow in the PATH, or gyroflow_app set

No motion data? Gyroflow

Ctrl+e

A clip the player doesn't stabilize by itself opens in Gyroflow as it is. One with no motion data (DJI with EIS on, or a camera that records none) is stabilized there from the picture (optical flow); a GoPro or Insta360 clip shot with the camera's stabilization on has its motion data, and Gyroflow loads it. Save the project there, and the clip plays stabilized with it. The side panel has it as Stabilize in Gyroflow…

Under water

In-camera stabilization gives up under water (DJI's EIS, as tested), likely because water bends light and the lens then acts like a longer one than it's tuned for. Turn it off, and the clip plays steady here: the default preset is made for footage under water, corrected for how water bends light. On a test clip, the jitter between frames went from 0.16 px to 0.02 px.

Lens in or out of the water

Ctrl+u · panel → Source

Source → Under water, flat port: Auto (the default), Yes or No, for above water or behind a dome port. Auto follows the camera's In water / Out of water over the clip, changing over one frame; for a camera that doesn't record it, the preset's (under water, by default). It's how the clip is read, so Save to the source keeps it for that clip. A Gyroflow project sets it itself, and it needs a known lens. Ctrl+u goes from under water to above water, and back.

Following the camera: the Osmo Action 6, the only camera that records it.

Edges filled

Ctrl+b · panel → Performance & Quality

What fills the parts the stabilized frame doesn't cover, blurred and blended in:

  • Trail by direction, the default: what was last seen in that direction of the world, so it lines up with the picture through a pan; where nothing was seen yet, the colors around spread in;
  • Frame's edge spread out: only the frame's own colors, spread out from its edge;
  • Background color: the project's background color, as Gyroflow renders it.

Starts at once

A clip without a project plays from its first seconds of motion data and reads the rest as it plays: from a NAS in about a second, where reading all of it first took 5-10 s.

On a slower computer

panel → Performance & Quality

When the stabilization keeps dropping frames for more than 2 s, playback pauses and offers a lower frame rate, resolution or edge fill, or skipping the color aberration correction on this screen. Performance & Quality in the side panel sets them.

360 video

Look around inside a 360° clip as it plays: drag, or move two fingers on a trackpad. The horizon stays level. This is the first version, tried on a Mac.

360 video, explained step by step →

Straight from the camera

An Insta360 X5's own .insv, with both lenses in one file, is stitched into one sphere as it plays, by Gyroflow's engine, with the rolling shutter's wobble taken out. The camera's gyro turns the sphere back as the camera tilts, so the horizon stays level wherever you look. Clips over 30 fps play every other frame.

Verified Insta360 X5, on one clip so far Should work Insta360 X4 Not yet Insta360 X3 (a file per lens); DJI Osmo 360's own .OSV; GoPro MAX 2's own .360

Clips already stitched

An MP4 or MOV with 360 metadata, as Insta360 Studio, DJI Studio or GoPro Player export it, is built to open the same way. It has no motion data any more, so it plays as it was stitched, and only your view turns.

Should work stitched 360 clips (tried on made-up ones only)

Looking around

Alt+v · Alt+p · panel → 360° view

The view turns on by itself when a 360 clip opens, playing or paused. Drag or two fingers look around, the wheel or a pinch zooms, Alt+drag or turning two fingers tilts the horizon, and a double click or Backspace looks ahead again. Alt+v shows the whole sphere unrolled flat; Alt+p goes through the projections: Auto, Rectilinear, Stereographic and Little planet.

What straight ahead follows

Alt+h · panel → 360° view

Camera: where the camera points, smoothed over a few seconds (the default without GPS). World: where it looked at the start, held. Way: along the way you move, from the GPS track. North: true north, from the GPS track. Way and North need a clip recorded with GPS; modes the clip can't give are greyed, with the reason.

Verified Camera and World, on the Insta360 X5 clip Should work Way and North: tested on made-up motion only, not yet on a real clip with GPS

360 exports Coming soon

The whole sphere as a level 360 video, or a normal 16:9 video along where you looked, saved from the player like other clips.

Telemetry

What the camera measured, over the video, read from the clip when it opens.

On screen

Ctrl+t

Each field shows when the clip has it: the depth and In water / Out of water on a DJI clip; on a GoPro the position and speed once the GPS has a fix, or the exposure on one without GPS. Ctrl+t turns it off and on; it sticks for the next clips.

Verified the depth, in water, on the Osmo Action 6 Should work its other fields (no GPS fix seen yet); Osmo 360; GoPro GPS, speed and exposure Not yet other cameras

Depth

Under water, from the barometer, in 0.1 m steps about once a second. In a dive housing it isn't recorded (the barometer measures the air in the housing), but In water still shows, from the picture.

More fields

panel → Telemetry

There for the asking: pressure, the altitude out of water (relative, so a rough guide), the exposure (ISO, shutter, aperture, white balance), and on an Osmo 360 the image sensor's temperature and the heart rate from a paired device.

Where, and in which units

Any corner of the video (bottom left by default), metric or imperial (feet, mph, °F), from the menu or the side panel.

Highlights & parts

The camera's highlights

Ctrl+] / Ctrl+[

Press the camera's highlight button while it records: the marks show on the seek bar, and the keys jump to the next one and back, landing two seconds before it, since the moment has usually happened by the time you press. Page Up / Page Down go to the mark itself.

Verified DJI Osmo Action 6 (the quick switch button, or the GPS Bluetooth remote) Should work DJI Osmo Action 4 and Action 5 Pro; GoPro HERO5 and later (HiLight, "GoPro, HiLight", or the remote) The Osmo 360 and the Pocket 3 don't mark highlights.

All of them, by time

panel → Source

The side panel's Source → Marks has every mark, with its time: a click jumps there. Without the panel, the plugins' menu lists them by time.

Mark a part

l twice

At the start of the part and at its end (mpv's A-B loop): what Shift+E then exports, and k saves as a moment.

Moments, marks & projects

What you keep from a trip, as plain files in its folder that Finder showsFile Explorer shows and your editor takes in. The story, from the card to the edit: Moments and marks.

Projects, moments and marks, explained step by step →

Save a moment

k

Mark a part with l twice, then k saves it as a moment, after asking for its name. The field is empty, with a suggestion shown: Enter takes it, typing replaces it, Esc cancels. A moment you don't name is named after its date and time.

A moment is a small file

Each moment is a small MP4 in the project's _Moments/ folder, under its name: <name>.afplink.mp4. Finder's thumbnails and Quick Look show itFile Explorer's thumbnails and its preview pane (Alt+P) show it in color, not log grey. Opened in the player, it plays that part of the original, in full quality, shown as it was when you saved it: its whole transform, the stabilization and the frame included.

Its icon is the first mark inside it, else its middle frame. Its file dates are the moment's real time, so sorting by date puts the moments in the order they happened.

Sort them like any files

Deleting the file deletes the moment. Subfolders of _Moments/ are yours, to sort moments into; renaming a file renames the moment.

Settings of its own

panel → Transform

A moment is made with what plays, and a copy of the clip's Gyroflow project when it has one. It never changes by itself: what you change while it plays is for viewing until Save to the moment. Switch Settings of its own off, and it plays as its clip is saved. A clip preview is the other way round: it plays as its clip, until you switch it on.

Marks

M

Points in a clip, on the seek bar. M adds a mark where the video is; on a mark, it removes it. The marks are the camera's highlights until you change one: your first change saves all of them to the clip's own small file, <clip>.afp.json, which also keeps how you saved the clip to play. Delete that file, and the camera's marks are back, and the clip forgets how you saved it. A <clip>.marks.json from an earlier version is read, and becomes .afp.json at the next change.

The trip at a glance

Action Footage Library

Made when you ask, with Make clip previews in Action Footage Library, opened from the side panel's Project tab: a _Clips/ folder with one small MP4 per clip, a flip-book of the clip in color. In icon view the folder is the whole trip in color instead of a wall of grey log, and Quick Look flips through each clip in seconds.With large icons the folder is the whole trip in color instead of a wall of grey log, and the preview pane (Alt+P) flips through each clip in seconds.

Set a clip aside

X

Not interesting: X in the player, or deleting the clip's flip-book from _Clips/, which is the same thing. The clip itself stays where it is.

The whole trip at once

Action Footage Library

What you do to one clip happens in the player; what you do to the whole trip happens in Action Footage Library, a small second app that comes with the player. It makes the trip's batches in the background, one at a time: clip previews, moments and photos of all marks, and exports of all moments. Open it with Open in Action Footage Library in the side panel's Project tab, or double-click the trip's Project.afproject.

Mac, Windows not on Linux yet

Favorites and ratings Coming soon

K

A moment or a clip's flip-book that exists is kept; one deleted is not. K makes the moment playing, else the clip, a favorite, and again takes it off: a green Favorite tag in Findera rating of 4 stars or more in File Explorer. Your editor gets them too.

Cut out a spoiled part

Ctrl+x

Mark the part with l twice, press Ctrl+x and confirm. The clip is rewritten without that part, without re-encoding: what's kept is copied as it was, and checked before anything is replaced. A part in the middle leaves two clips; the second starts at the keyframe at or before the cut's end, so up to about a second of the spoiled part can stay.

The original goes to the Trash, with its marks file, Gyroflow project and clip preview. Marks and moments move to the new clips, and each new clip gets the clip's saved transform; exports aren't touched. There's no undo in the player. No project needed.

DJI clips; GoPro clips when the check passes. Not Insta360 or iPhone clips, moments, clip previews or exports.

Project folders

panel → Project

A project is a trip's folder, set up once: the first time you save a moment or export, or open Action Footage Library, the side panel's Project tab asks which folder, the clips' own or one above it, and Initialize the project here makes it. Every folder inside belongs to the project. In it go _Moments/, _Clips/, _Exports/ and _Stills/, and the file Project.afproject, which opens the trip in Action Footage Library. A project made by an earlier version has an _ActionFootagePlayer/ folder instead: the player moves it into Project.afproject the next time it opens one of its clips. Each clip's .afp.json and Gyroflow project stay beside it. When that folder can't be written to (a locked card, a read-only share), the player asks for a name and makes a project folder in its folder in Documents (Documents/ActionFootagePlayer/), with a Sources.txt that points to the footage. Moments and marks

Exporting & sharing

What each look exports, explained step by step →

Export what you see

Shift+E

The clip as it plays, in one pass: stabilized or not, with the same transform (view, level, horizon, lens and projection, frame) and edges, in the same look. With a part marked, only that part; the side panel's Output → Export has Mark start, Mark end and Export the clip or Export the part a–b. Shift+E twice on a clip being exported cancels it.

Verified HDR exports from the Osmo Action 6 Should work Rec.709 exports; the other cameras (any camera Gyroflow supports: with a project)

A file for your phone

Log clips export as HEVC 10-bit in HDR (Rec.2100 HLG), tagged so that macOS, iOS and YouTube show them as HDR. Exported on a Mac in an HLG+DV look, they also carry Dolby Vision metadata for Apple devices (see HDR on any screen). NBCU's and the camera maker's SDR looks export Rec.709 instead, and so does Export in SDR in the side panel's Output → Export, whatever plays: for log clips, and for files already in HDR (HLG or PQ), through NBCU's conversion. SDR tone mapped by mpv exports HLG, and SDR, HLG+DV exports HLG with Dolby Vision. 10-bit stays 10-bit.

Where it goes

The project's _Exports/ folder, under the name you give it in the same dialog as a moment's, with the look in brackets unless the export is HDR, HLG: Turtle.mov, Turtle (SDR).mov, Turtle (Dolby Vision).mov. It carries a link to the original: without the side panel, the right-click menu's Open the original plays its part of the clip. The audio is copied as it is and the file keeps the clip's dates. A notification says where it went, and Show the last export, in the side panel's Output → Export, shows it in FinderFile Explorer.

Keep watching meanwhile

Playback goes on during an export, with its progress in the top right corner; several clips can export at once. On a Mac, an export runs at background priority while the video plays, and nearly stops: pause, and it goes on at full speed.

Stabilize + LUT in Finder's Quick ActionsFile Explorer's right-click menu exports the selected videos, log as HDR (HLG), into the project's _Exports/ folder, or a stabilized/ folder next to the clip when there's no project. So does the command line, which can also write ProRes 422 HQ for editing. Action Footage Library exports every moment of a trip not exported yet, each with its own settings.

Clean still frame & photos

Photos from your video, explained step by step →

Temporal denoise of the paused frame

Ctrl+p

A moment after the video pauses, the frame is rebuilt from it and the 5 frames on each side of it, each moved onto it by the motion between them: the sensor's noise from dim light mostly goes (temporal noise reduction), and the detail stays. About a second for 4K; the stabilization must be on. It's on by default, so pausing is enough; Ctrl+p turns it off, and on again, for the next clips too.

Paused frame at full size

Ctrl+f

The paused frame at the source's own resolution, whatever the playback settings, cleaned up in the same pass when Ctrl+p is on. In the side panel, Performance & Quality → Paused frame → Full size does it after each pause.

Save the frame as a photo

Alt+s · c · panel → Save frame

Rendered again from the original file at full resolution, stabilized and cleaned, with a gain map for HDR screens, into the project's _Stills/. c frames it first: its shape, and on a stabilized clip a turn of the camera and a level horizon.

Live Photos and Motion Photos

panel → Save frame → Photo

The photo with the 3 s of video around it: a Live Photo in the Mac app, a Motion Photo on a Mac and on Windows. Plain clips work too, ones not in log and without motion data. A Motion Photo's video is SDR, in the photo's own look, so it matches the photo in Android's and Windows' photo apps.

Noise reduction & under-water color

Noise reduction

Ctrl+p

The paused frame and the photos you save are cleaned, rebuilt from the frames around them: the clean still frame. The noise reduction while playing has no key, menu or switch any more; denoise=yes in dlogm.conf still turns it on.

Color fringes under water

Ctrl+a · panel → Transform → Water

Water bends blue more than red, so behind the camera's flat glass red and blue land apart towards the edges of the frame. Wherever the lens is under water, each color is moved back to where its own ray lands, from physics alone, nothing fitted to footage; above water nothing changes. Transform → Water → Reduce color aberration (on by default, Ctrl+a) leaves the fringes as recorded, and takes them out again; it's part of the clip's transform. On a slower computer, Performance & Quality → Skip color aberration correction leaves it out on this screen only. Playback only.

On Osmo Action 6 dive clips, red's fringe of 3-7 px at the edges goes to within about a pixel.

Side panel & menu

The side panel

Tab

The settings, in a panel at the right of the window, beside the video, which moves over for it; the keys are beside what they do. Tab hides it and shows it again.

  • Project, where the panel opens: the trip's project folder (Initialize the project here), Open in Action Footage Library, this clip's marks and moments, marking a part and saving it as a moment;
  • Source: what the clip is, FinderFile Explorer and its path, how to read it (the log curve, how it was recorded turned, its lens, under water with a flat port) with Save to the source and Revert to saved, and its marks;
  • Transform: where it's defined, Save, Revert to saved, Use as default, Auto save and Settings of its own; the stabilization, the level, the horizon, the lens and its projection, the frame's shape and orientation, the color fringes under water; or, with a Gyroflow project, the project's values;
  • Output: Display, the looks; Export, the clip or the marked part, Export in SDR, the last export;
  • Save frame: framing a photo, its shape, the camera, the kind of photo;
  • Performance & Quality: the frame rate and size it's stabilized at, the paused frame, the edge fill, skipping the color aberration correction on this screen, whether it asks when the GPU can't keep up;
  • Telemetry: whether it shows, the corner, the units, the fields;
  • Dolby Vision (the Mac app): the trims, as sliders;
  • 360° view, while a 360 clip plays: the projection, the view and what it follows;
  • Support: the version, Keys…, Note for the agent…, Report this camera…, Set up again…, and links to the website and GitHub.

Source and Output end with In the folder: Previous clip, Next clip, Autoplay next and Loop the clip.

Platforms & install

Mac with Apple Silicon

In daily use

macOS 15 or later. The app carries its own mpv and ffmpeg: nothing else to install. It's signed ad hoc, so the first time: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Its setup makes it the player for MP4 videos, adds the Quick Actions and downloads DJI's LUT. The download holds two apps, side by side: the player, ActionFootagePlayer.app, and Action Footage Library, ActionFootageLibrary.app.

Windows 10 or 11

Alpha

An app of its own, for x86-64 and for Windows 11 on ARM, with everything in it, Action Footage Library too: ActionFootageLibrary.exe in the player's folder, with its own Start menu entry, and Project.afproject files open in it. The x86-64 app has been tried and works, with little testing so far; the ARM one is only checked by the build. It installs for you alone, with no admin rights; SmartScreen warns the first time: More info → Run anyway.

Linux

Untried

The plugins, in an mpv of your own: built by every release, not tried yet. The stabilization needs an mpv built with VapourSynth. Action Footage Library isn't on Linux yet, so the batches for a whole trip aren't there; the player does the rest one clip at a time.

The plugins in your own mpv

On any system: log in color, telemetry, highlights and the menu are plain Lua and GPU shaders. The stabilization and the export need an mpv built with VapourSynth, and come built for macOS on Apple Silicon, Linux x86-64 and Windows. Nothing goes in mpv.conf or input.conf.

Downloads and install steps

Options for advanced users

Each plugin reads a <plugin>.conf in the app's mpv/script-opts/ folder, in ~/Library/Application Support/ActionFootagePlayer/ (Windows: %LOCALAPPDATA%\ActionFootagePlayer\). Use as default and Make these the defaults write them for you. A few of them:

Settings files

dlogm.conf
hdr_screen=hdr and sdr_screen=dv: the look log clips start in on HDR screens and on screens without HDR (hdr, dv, dvsdr, tonemap, nbcu or maker); denoise=yes: the noise reduction while playing, on (it has no key or menu any more); dv_live_l1=no (the Mac app): the trims' fixed light levels instead of each frame's
gyroflow.conf
view, level, horizon_lock, chromatic, aspect, orientation, projection, lens: the transform clips with nothing saved start with (Use as default writes them); underwater: how a lens is read until a clip's own is saved; edges: for every clip; chromatic_skip: skip the color aberration correction on this screen; resolution, fps_limit, edge_fill_rate, ask_when_slow: for slower computers; still_radius, paused_full_size: the clean still frame; gyroflow_app: the Gyroflow app to open (Windows, Linux)
dji_telemetry.conf
fields, units, position, font_size, visible
highlights.conf
before=2: seconds before a highlight a jump lands; chapters: the marks on the seek bar

Your own default preset

Clips without a project get Gyroflow's default preset, then a default.gyroflow of yours, put in the app's gyroflow/ folder.
